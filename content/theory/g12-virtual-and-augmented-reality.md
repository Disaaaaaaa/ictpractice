# Virtual and augmented reality
@lo 12.4.3.2

:::cards
### Virtual reality (VR)
A fully **computer-generated 3D world** that replaces what the user sees and hears. The user is **immersed** and can look around and interact.
### Augmented reality (AR)
**Computer-generated images, text or sounds are added on top of the real world**, seen through a phone, tablet or smart glasses.
### Mixed reality (MR)
Virtual objects are **anchored in the real world** and the user can interact with them as if they were real (e.g. Microsoft HoloLens, Apple Vision Pro).
:::

:::compare Hardware
| VR | AR |
|---|---|
| Head-mounted display (headset) with a screen for each eye | Smartphone/tablet camera and screen |
| Motion sensors: gyroscope, accelerometer, head and hand tracking | Camera + GPS + compass + accelerometer to know position and direction |
| Hand controllers, data gloves, haptic (vibration) feedback | Smart glasses (head-up display) |
| Powerful computer / GPU to render the scene | Software that recognises surfaces and markers |
:::

## Purposes and applications

:::compare
| Area | VR | AR |
|---|---|---|
| Education | Virtual field trips (space, history, inside the human body); virtual laboratories with dangerous experiments | 3D models appear over the textbook; labels on real objects |
| Training | Flight and driving simulators; surgery practice; firefighter and military training — safe, repeatable, cheap per session | Step-by-step repair instructions over a real engine |
| Medicine | Treating phobias and PTSD; pain distraction; rehabilitation games | Showing veins or organs over the patient during surgery |
| Architecture and design | Walking through a building before it is built | Seeing new furniture in your own room (IKEA Place) |
| Retail and tourism | Virtual stores and museums | Virtual try-on of glasses/clothes; information about sights through the camera |
| Entertainment | Immersive games, virtual concerts | Pokémon GO, Snapchat filters |
| Navigation | — | Arrows drawn over the street view; head-up displays in cars |
:::

:::compare Advantages and disadvantages
| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Safe practice of dangerous or expensive tasks | VR headsets are expensive and need powerful hardware |
| Makes abstract ideas visible and memorable | Motion sickness, eye strain, headaches |
| Remote collaboration and visits without travel | Isolation from real people (VR); distraction and accidents (AR) |
| Engaging and motivating for learners | Content is expensive to create; privacy of camera data |
:::

:::tip
"Explain the purpose" — say **what problem it solves**: "VR lets trainee pilots **practise emergency situations safely** without risking an aircraft or lives."
:::
