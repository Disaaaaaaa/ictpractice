# Intellectual property
@lo 12.1.3.4

:::definition Intellectual property (IP)
Creations of the **mind** — inventions, software, books, music, films, designs, brand names and logos — that the **creator owns** and has the legal right to control, even though they are not physical objects.
:::

:::definition Copyright
The **legal right of the author** of an **original work** (program code, text, music, film, photo, website) to decide who may **copy, distribute, change or sell** it. It arises **automatically** when the work is created — no registration is needed.
:::

:::definition Patent
A **registered right** that gives the inventor the **exclusive right to make, use and sell an invention** (a new technical solution, device or process) for a limited time (about **20 years**), in return for publishing how it works.
:::

:::definition Trademark
A **registered sign** — name, logo, slogan — that **identifies the products of one company** and distinguishes them from others (™ = claimed, ® = registered).
:::

:::compare Ways intellectual property is protected
| Protection | What it protects | How it is obtained | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copyright** © | **Original works**: software code, texts, music, films, photos, websites | **Automatically** when the work is created; lasts the author's life + 70 years (in Kazakhstan and many countries) | The code of a mobile game; a song |
| **Patent** | **Inventions** — new technical solutions, devices, processes | **Applied for** and registered; lasts about 20 years | A new type of battery |
| **Trademark** ™ ® | **Brand names, logos, slogans** that identify a company's products | **Registered** | The logo of a Kazakh bank or airline |
| **Industrial design** | The **appearance** of a product | Registered | The shape of a phone |
| **Trade secret** | Confidential business information | Kept secret (contracts, NDAs) | A recipe, an algorithm |
:::

:::callout info The law in Kazakhstan
The **Law on Copyright and Related Rights** protects works including **computer programs and databases**. Intellectual property is also protected by the **Civil Code**, and serious infringement is a criminal offence.
:::

:::definition Software licence
A **legal agreement** between the owner of the software and the user that states **how the software may be used** — on how many devices, whether it may be copied, shared or changed, and whether the source code is available.
:::

:::compare Software licences
| Licence | Can you use it? | Can you copy / share it? | Source code available? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Proprietary (commercial)** | After **paying** for a licence | **No** | No | Microsoft Office, Adobe Photoshop |
| **Shareware / trial** | Free for a **trial period**, then pay | Often the trial only | No | WinRAR, many games |
| **Freeware** | **Free** | Usually yes, but you may not change it | No | Many free utilities and players |
| **Open source** | Free | **Yes**, and you may **modify** it (under the licence terms, e.g. GPL) | **Yes** | Linux, LibreOffice, Firefox |
| **Creative Commons** (for content) | Free, under conditions: attribution (BY), non-commercial (NC), no derivatives (ND), share-alike (SA) | As the licence allows | — | Wikipedia images, many photos |
:::

:::warning Common mistakes about IP
- Thinking that anything on the Internet is **free to use** — most content is protected by copyright **automatically**.
- Confusing **freeware** (free, closed source) with **open source** (source available, can be modified).
- Saying a trademark protects an invention — it protects a **brand**; an invention is protected by a **patent**.
- Saying copyright must be **registered** — it is automatic.
:::

# Problems caused by disseminating and using information
@lo 12.1.3.4

Digital information can be **copied and shared instantly, perfectly and almost free of charge**. This brings huge benefits, but also problems for creators, individuals and society.

:::definition Piracy
**Copying, distributing, selling or downloading** software, films, music or books **without the permission** of the copyright owner (without a licence).
:::

:::definition Plagiarism
Presenting **someone else's work, words or ideas as your own** without acknowledging (citing) the source.
:::

:::definition Misinformation and disinformation
**Misinformation** is false or misleading information spread **by mistake**; **disinformation** is false information spread **deliberately** to deceive (fake news).
:::

:::compare Problems of disseminating and using information
| Problem | What happens | Who is harmed |
|---|---|---|
| **Software / media piracy** | Copying, sharing or selling software, films, music or books **without permission** | Creators and companies lose **income**; less money for new products; users risk **malware** in pirated copies |
| **Plagiarism** | Presenting someone else's work or ideas **as your own** without acknowledging the source | The original author; the plagiarist's learning and reputation; academic honesty |
| **Copyright infringement online** | Using images, music or videos in a website or video without a licence | Creators; the user may be **fined** or have content removed |
| **Misinformation and fake news** | False or misleading information spreads quickly on social media | Public **health**, elections, trust in media; people make wrong decisions |
| **Disclosure of personal data** | Personal information is published or shared without consent (doxxing, leaked databases) | **Privacy**; identity theft, harassment |
| **Defamation** | False statements that damage someone's reputation | The person or company targeted |
| **Harmful and illegal content** | Hate speech, extremist material, cyberbullying | Victims; social cohesion |
| **Information overload / unreliable sources** | Too much information of mixed quality | Users find it hard to decide what is **true** |
:::

:::steps How to analyse a problem (exam answers)
- **Name** the problem precisely (e.g. "plagiarism of a research project").
- Say **what happens** — how the information is spread or used.
- Say **who is harmed** — the creator, the user, other people, society.
- Give the **consequence** for each (loss of income, malware, wrong decisions, loss of trust…).
- Suggest a **solution or prevention** (licence, citation, fact-checking, consent).
:::

:::cards
### Analysing a problem — piracy
- **For the creator:** loss of sales and royalties; less money to pay developers.
- **For the user:** legal risk (fines), **no updates or support**, pirated copies often contain **malware**.
- **For society:** fewer new products, lower investment in the IT industry, lost tax revenue.
- **Ethically:** it is using someone else's work **without paying** for it.
- **Prevention:** licence keys and online activation, copy protection (DRM), legal action to remove illegal copies, affordable prices / subscriptions and free demos.
### Analysing a problem — fake news
- Spreads **faster** than corrections because it is shocking or emotional.
- Can cause **panic**, damage reputations, influence **elections** or harm health (false cures).
- Makes people **distrust** all media, including reliable sources.
- **Solutions:** check the source, compare with other reliable sources, look at the date, use fact-checking sites, think before sharing.
:::

:::compare How to use information legally and ethically
| Situation | Correct behaviour |
|---|---|
| Using a photo from the Internet in a project | Use **copyright-free / Creative Commons** images and **credit** the author, or ask permission |
| Quoting a book or website in an essay | Use **quotation marks** and **cite the source** (author, title, date, URL) |
| Installing software | Buy a **licence** or use **free / open-source** alternatives |
| Sharing a news story | **Check** that it comes from a reliable source before sharing |
| Posting about other people | Get their **consent**; do not share personal data |
:::

:::example Worked example — a school documentary
Students of grade 12 make a video about Almaty for a competition. They plan to use (1) a popular song, (2) photos found with a search engine, (3) a paragraph copied from a travel website.
- **Song:** protected by copyright → use **royalty-free / Creative Commons** music or ask the rights holder; otherwise the video may be removed and the school fined.
- **Photos:** search-engine results are **not** free to use → filter by **Creative Commons** licence and **credit** each author, or take their own photos.
- **Text:** copying it without credit is **plagiarism** → write it in their own words and **cite** the website, or quote it in quotation marks with the source.
:::

:::callout info Fair use / exceptions
Copyright law allows limited use without permission for **education, criticism, news reporting and quotation**, as long as the source is acknowledged and only a **reasonable part** is used.
:::

# Exam practice: intellectual property and information problems
@lo 12.1.3.4

:::example Exam-style answers
**Q: Define the term intellectual property. [1]**
Creations of the mind (inventions, software, texts, music, designs, logos) that the creator owns and has legal rights over (1).

**Q: Explain the difference between copyright and a patent. [2]**
Copyright protects **original works** such as software code and texts and is **automatic** (1); a patent protects an **invention** and must be **applied for and registered** (1).

**Q: Analyse one problem that arises when software is copied illegally. [3]**
The developer **loses income** from sales (1), so it has less money to employ programmers and develop updates or new products (1); users of pirated copies risk **malware** and get no support or updates (1).

**Q: Explain the difference between freeware and open-source software. [2]**
Freeware is free to use but the **source code is not available / cannot be changed** (1); open-source software makes the **source code available and allows it to be modified** and shared (1).

**Q: State two ways a student can avoid plagiarism. [2]**
Cite / reference every source (author, title, URL, date) (1); put ideas in their own words / use quotation marks for exact quotes (1).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- Writing "it is illegal" as the only problem of piracy — **analyse**: who loses what, and why.
- Mixing up plagiarism (claiming authorship) and piracy (copying without a licence).
- Writing "check the information" — say **how**: the source, other reliable sources, the date.
:::

:::steps Check yourself
- What is intellectual property? Name four ways to protect it and what each protects.
- Which protection is automatic, and which must be registered?
- Compare proprietary, shareware, freeware and open-source licences.
- Analyse the effects of piracy on creators, users and society.
- How can you check whether a news story is fake?
- How should you use images and quotations from the Internet in a project?
:::

# E-government resources
@lo 12.1.3.7

:::definition E-government (eGov)
The use of the **Internet and digital technologies** by the state to provide **public services** to citizens and businesses **online**. In Kazakhstan the main portal is **egov.kz** with the **eGov mobile** app.
:::

:::definition Public service (online)
A service provided by a state body to a citizen or business — a certificate, a registration, a payment, an application — which can be **requested and received through the eGov portal** without visiting an office.
:::

:::compare Examples of eGov services in Kazakhstan
| Service | What the citizen can do online |
|---|---|
| **Certificates** | Get a certificate of address registration, no criminal record, birth or marriage certificate |
| **Registration** | Register a place of residence, a car, a business (individual entrepreneur) |
| **Education** | Apply to a kindergarten or school, see UNT results, apply for grants |
| **Health** | Attach to a clinic, book a doctor's appointment |
| **Taxes and payments** | Pay taxes, fines, utility bills |
| **Social payments** | Apply for benefits and pensions, check pension savings (ENPF) |
| **Digital documents** | Show an ID card or driving licence from the phone instead of the paper document |
| **Licences and permits** | Apply through **elicense.kz** |
:::

:::example Worked example — a certificate for a school application
Mr Seitkali needs a **certificate of address registration** for his son's school application.
1. He opens **egov.kz** (typing the address himself) or the **eGov mobile** app and logs in with his **EDS** (or password + one-time SMS code).
2. He finds *Certificate of address registration* in the service catalogue.
3. His name, IIN and address are **filled in automatically** from the state databases; he checks them.
4. He **signs** the request with his EDS / SMS code.
5. A few minutes later the certificate appears as a **PDF with a QR code** in his personal account; the school can check it by scanning the QR code.
He saved a trip to a public service centre and a queue — and paid nothing.
:::

:::compare Advantages and disadvantages of e-government
| Advantages | Disadvantages / risks |
|---|---|
| Services available **24/7** from anywhere | Needs **Internet access**, a device and digital skills — the **digital divide** (elderly, rural areas) |
| **Saves time** — no queues, no travel | Risk of **cyber-attacks** and leaks of citizens' personal data |
| **Less paperwork**; documents checked automatically between databases | **Phishing** — fake eGov sites and SMS messages that steal passwords or EDS |
| **Less corruption** — fewer face-to-face contacts with officials | Technical failures make services unavailable |
| **Transparent** — the citizen can track the status of an application | People may lose or share their EDS key and password |
:::

# Using eGov safely: digital signature and security
@lo 12.1.3.7 12.1.3.4

:::definition Electronic digital signature (EDS / ЭЦП)
A **cryptographic key pair** (a private key kept by the owner, protected by a password, and a public certificate issued by the National Certification Authority) used to **sign electronic documents**. It **proves who signed** the document and **shows that it has not been changed** after signing; it has the same legal force as a handwritten signature.
:::

:::warning Using eGov safely
- Type **egov.kz** yourself or use the official app — do not follow links in unexpected messages.
- **Never share** your EDS password or SMS codes with anyone (officials never ask for them).
- Keep the EDS file on a protected device and **log out** on shared computers.
- Check that the site uses **HTTPS** and the correct address.
- Do not let other people sign documents with **your** EDS — whatever is signed with it is legally yours.
:::

:::cards
### Personal data on eGov
- The portal holds **sensitive personal data** — IIN, address, family, income, health records.
- A leak can lead to **identity theft**, fraud (loans taken in someone's name) and loss of privacy.
- Citizens should **check** the notifications about who requested their data, and report anything unusual.
### Who is left behind?
- The **digital divide**: elderly people, people in remote villages, people without smartphones.
- Solutions: **public service centres (ЦОН)** with assistants, self-service terminals, training courses, simple mobile apps.
:::

:::example Exam-style answers
**Q: Describe what is meant by e-government. [2]**
The state uses the Internet / digital technology (1) to provide public services to citizens and businesses online, e.g. egov.kz (1).

**Q: Give two advantages of using e-government services. [2]**
Services are available **24/7** without visiting an office (1); **saves time** / no queues / less paperwork / less corruption (1).

**Q: Explain the purpose of an electronic digital signature. [2]**
It **confirms the identity** of the person signing (1) and shows that the document **has not been changed** after it was signed (1).

**Q: Describe how a citizen receives a certificate through egov.kz. [3]**
Logs in with an EDS / password + SMS (1); chooses the service and checks the automatically filled form (1); signs the request and receives the certificate (PDF with QR code) in the personal account (1).

**Q: Explain one risk of e-government and how to reduce it. [2]**
Phishing — fake sites / messages steal the EDS password or SMS code (1); type the address yourself / use the official app and never share codes (1).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- Listing eGov services without saying what the **benefit** is.
- Thinking an EDS is a picture of your handwritten signature — it is a **cryptographic key**.
- Giving "hackers" as a risk without saying **what** can happen (leak of personal data, identity theft).
- Forgetting the **digital divide** when evaluating e-government.
:::
