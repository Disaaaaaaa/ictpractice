# AI and where it is applied
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::definition Artificial intelligence (AI)
The ability of computer systems to perform tasks that normally require **human intelligence**: perceiving (seeing, hearing), understanding language, reasoning, **learning from data**, planning and making decisions.
:::

AI is used wherever there is **a lot of data**, a task that must be done **quickly, accurately or repeatedly**, or a job that is **dangerous** for people. To describe an application in the exam, say **what the AI does**, **how** (what data or technique), and **why it helps**.

:::compare Main AI capabilities used in applications
| Capability | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Computer vision** | Recognising objects, faces and defects in images and video | Detecting a tumour on an MRI scan |
| **Natural language processing (NLP)** | Understanding and producing human language | Chatbots, translation, voice assistants |
| **Prediction / machine learning** | Finding patterns in past data to predict the future | Predicting when a machine will break down |
| **Planning and decision-making** | Choosing the best action to reach a goal | Route planning, game opponents |
| **Robotics** | Controlling machines that sense and act in the physical world | Warehouse robots, surgical robots |
| **Recommendation** | Suggesting items a user is likely to want | Films, products, learning resources |
:::

# AI in industry
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::cards
### Robotics and automation
**Industrial robots** with AI vision weld, paint, assemble and pack products on production lines. **Collaborative robots (cobots)** work safely next to people, slowing down when a worker comes close.
### Predictive maintenance
Sensors on machines send data (vibration, temperature, noise). AI learns the patterns that come **before a breakdown** and warns engineers to repair the machine **before it fails** — avoiding expensive stoppages.
### Quality control
Cameras and **computer vision** inspect every product (bottles, chips, car parts) and reject faulty ones faster and more reliably than human inspectors.
### Logistics and supply chain
AI **forecasts demand**, plans stock levels and delivery routes; robots in **warehouses** pick and move goods (e.g. Amazon). Self-driving trucks and drones deliver goods.
### Energy and resources
AI controls heating, lighting and machines to **reduce energy use**; in mining and oil it analyses geological data to find deposits.
### Design
**Generative design** — AI proposes thousands of shapes for a part that is strong but light; engineers choose the best.
:::

:::compare AI in industry — benefits and risks
| Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|
| Higher **productivity**; machines work 24/7 | **Job losses** for manual and routine workers |
| More consistent **quality**, fewer defects | High **cost** of robots and AI systems |
| Fewer accidents — robots do **dangerous** tasks | Breakdowns or **cyber-attacks** can stop production |
| Less waste and energy use | Workers need **retraining** for new skills |
:::

# AI in education
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::cards
### Adaptive (personalised) learning
The platform analyses each student's answers and chooses the **next task at the right level**, repeating topics the student finds hard (e.g. Duolingo, Khan Academy, this exam-prep platform's AI marking).
### Intelligent tutoring systems and chatbots
AI tutors **explain**, give **hints** and answer students' questions at any time; chatbots answer common questions from students and parents.
### Automatic marking and feedback
AI marks tests, essays and code and gives **instant feedback**, saving teachers' time.
### Learning analytics
AI finds students who are **falling behind** or may drop out, so teachers can help early.
### Accessibility
Speech-to-text, text-to-speech, automatic **subtitles** and **translation** help students with disabilities or a different home language.
### Academic integrity
AI tools detect **plagiarism** and copied code; exam proctoring software monitors online exams.
:::

:::compare AI in education — benefits and risks
| Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|
| Learning at the student's **own pace** and level | Students may **over-rely** on AI and not think for themselves; cheating with AI writing tools |
| **Instant feedback**, available 24/7 | AI marking may be **wrong** or unfair for unusual answers |
| Teachers save time on marking and admin | **Privacy** — large amounts of student data are collected |
| Early help for struggling students | Less **human contact**; AI cannot replace a teacher's empathy and motivation |
:::

# AI in medicine
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::cards
### Diagnosis and medical imaging
Deep-learning systems analyse **X-rays, CT/MRI scans, skin photos and eye scans** to detect cancer, fractures, pneumonia or diabetic eye disease — often as accurately as specialists and much faster.
### Drug discovery
AI predicts how molecules will behave and which could become **new medicines**, reducing years of laboratory work (e.g. predicting protein structures with AlphaFold).
### Personalised treatment
AI combines a patient's **medical history, genetics and test results** to suggest the most effective treatment and dose.
### Robot-assisted surgery
Surgeons control robotic arms that make **precise, steady** movements through small cuts; AI can guide and stabilise the instruments.
### Monitoring and prediction
**Wearables** (smart watches) track heart rate and detect irregular rhythms; hospital AI predicts which patients are at risk of getting worse; AI tracks the spread of **epidemics**.
### Administration and virtual assistants
Chatbots book appointments and check symptoms; NLP converts doctors' speech into medical notes.
:::

:::compare AI in medicine — benefits and risks
| Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|
| **Earlier, faster** diagnosis; fewer missed cases | A wrong diagnosis may harm the patient — **who is responsible**? |
| Specialist knowledge available in **rural areas** | Very **sensitive personal data** — privacy and security |
| Faster development of medicines | Bias if trained on data from only some groups of patients |
| Doctors spend less time on paperwork | Patients may not trust a "black box"; loss of human care |
:::

# AI in the gaming industry
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::cards
### Non-player characters (NPCs)
AI controls the behaviour of enemies, allies and crowds: they **react to the player**, take cover, work as a team and make decisions (often with **finite-state machines** or **behaviour trees**).
### Pathfinding
Algorithms such as **A\*** find the shortest route for characters around obstacles on the game map.
### Procedural content generation
AI **generates levels, maps, landscapes, quests and items** automatically, so every game is different (e.g. Minecraft worlds, No Man's Sky planets).
### Dynamic difficulty adjustment
The game watches how well the player is doing and makes it **easier or harder** to keep it challenging but fair.
### AI opponents that learn
Programs such as **AlphaGo** and chess engines learned by **reinforcement learning** to beat world champions; similar AI plays strategy games.
### Development and testing
AI bots **play-test** games to find bugs; AI generates graphics, animations and voices; **upscaling** (e.g. DLSS) makes graphics sharper at higher speed; AI detects **cheating** players.
:::

:::compare AI in gaming — benefits and risks
| Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|
| More **realistic, challenging** and varied games | Very **addictive** personalised games and in-game purchases |
| Personalised experience for each player | Collection of players' **behaviour data** |
| Cheaper, faster development and testing | Generated content may be repetitive or of low quality; artists' jobs |
| Better graphics on ordinary hardware | Cheating with AI aim-bots |
:::

# AI in society
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::compare AI in everyday life
| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Transport** | Self-driving cars and buses; traffic-light control; navigation apps that predict traffic jams |
| **Smart cities and homes** | Smart thermostats and lighting; energy grids that balance supply and demand; waste collection routes |
| **Communication** | Voice assistants; machine translation; spam filters; predictive text |
| **Finance** | Fraud detection on card payments; credit scoring; robo-advisers; banking chatbots |
| **Security and law enforcement** | Face recognition; detecting cyber-attacks; predicting crime hot spots |
| **Media and social networks** | Recommended posts, videos and music; content moderation; detecting fake news (and creating **deepfakes**) |
| **Environment and agriculture** | Weather and climate prediction; drones detecting crop disease; monitoring deforestation and wildlife |
| **Public services** | Chatbots for government services; predicting demand for hospitals and schools |
:::

:::cards
### Positive effects on society
- **Convenience** — services available 24/7, faster and personalised.
- **Safety** — fewer road accidents, fraud prevented, disasters predicted.
- **Accessibility** — translation, speech and image description help people with disabilities.
- **Economic growth** and new kinds of jobs (data scientists, AI engineers, ethicists).
- Help with global problems: climate, disease, food production.
### Negative effects and ethical issues
- **Unemployment** in routine jobs; growing **inequality**.
- **Privacy** and **mass surveillance**.
- **Bias and discrimination** in automated decisions.
- **Misinformation** — deepfakes and fake news; **filter bubbles** on social media.
- **Accountability** — who is responsible for an AI's harmful decision?
- **Security** — AI used for cyber-attacks or autonomous weapons.
- **Dependence** on AI and loss of human skills.
:::

:::callout info Using AI responsibly
Many countries are writing laws and guidelines for AI: decisions should be **transparent and explainable**, systems should be **tested for bias**, personal data must be **protected**, and a **human** should stay responsible for important decisions (medicine, justice, hiring).
:::

# Exam practice and summary
@lo 12.4.3.1

:::example Exam-style answers
**Q: Give one example of using AI in the following areas. [3]**
*Medicine:* disease diagnosis from medical images / drug discovery / personalised treatment plans (1).
*Gaming industry:* non-player characters that react to the player / procedural generation of levels / dynamic difficulty (1).
*Education:* adaptive learning that adjusts tasks to each student / automatic marking (1).

**Q: Describe how AI is used for predictive maintenance in industry. [2]**
Sensors collect data such as **vibration and temperature** from machines (1); AI learns the patterns that come **before a failure** and warns engineers so the machine is **repaired before it breaks** (1).

**Q: Describe one way AI is used in the gaming industry. [2]**
AI controls **non-player characters** (1) so that they **react to the player's actions**, e.g. enemies take cover or attack as a team, making the game more realistic (1).

**Q: Explain one negative impact of AI on society. [2]**
AI can **automate routine jobs** such as cashiers and drivers (1), so many people may become **unemployed** and need retraining (1).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- Naming an area with **no specific application**: "AI is used in medicine" — say **what it does** (e.g. *detects tumours on scans*).
- Giving a benefit without saying **why** it helps (faster → earlier treatment).
- Describing a **robot** that only repeats fixed movements as AI — AI is needed when it **senses, decides or learns**.
- Repeating the same example for two areas.
:::
