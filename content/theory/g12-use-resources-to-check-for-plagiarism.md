# Plagiarism
@lo 12.1.3.5

:::definition Plagiarism
**Presenting another person's work, words, ideas, images or code as your own**, without clearly **acknowledging the source**. It is a form of academic dishonesty and can also break **copyright** law.
:::

:::compare Forms of plagiarism
| Form | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Direct (copy-paste)** | Text is copied word for word without quotation marks or a reference |
| **Mosaic / patchwriting** | Phrases from several sources are mixed together with a few words changed |
| **Paraphrasing without citation** | Someone else's idea is rewritten in new words, but the source is not given |
| **Translation plagiarism** | A text in another language is translated and presented as original |
| **Self-plagiarism** | Re-using your own earlier work (e.g. a previous project) without permission or acknowledgement |
| **Code plagiarism** | Copying program code from a classmate or the Internet and changing variable names |
| **Ghost-writing / AI text** | Submitting work written by another person or by an AI tool as your own, when this is not allowed |
| **Incorrect citation** | Giving a wrong or incomplete source, so the reader cannot find the original |
:::

:::cards
### Why plagiarism is a problem
- It is **dishonest** — the work does not show the student's own knowledge.
- It is **unfair** to the original author and to other students.
- The student **does not learn** the skills the task was designed to develop.
- Consequences: **zero marks**, disqualification from an exam or competition, loss of reputation, expulsion; in professional life — legal action for copyright infringement.
### Why people plagiarise
- Lack of time / poor planning.
- Not knowing how to **cite** or **paraphrase** correctly.
- Thinking that information on the Internet is free to use.
- Pressure to get high marks.
:::

# How anti-plagiarism resources work
@lo 12.1.3.5

:::definition Anti-plagiarism resource
A program or online service that **compares a document with a very large database of texts** — web pages, books, journals and previously submitted student work — and produces a **similarity report** showing which parts match other sources.
:::

:::steps How a document is checked
- The user (or teacher) **uploads** the document (DOCX, PDF, TXT) or pastes the text.
- The system breaks the text into **fragments** (sequences of words, "shingles") and creates **digital fingerprints** of them.
- The fingerprints are **compared** with its databases: the Internet, academic publications, its archive of submitted papers.
- **Matching fragments are highlighted**, and each match is linked to its **source**.
- A **similarity (originality) score** in % is calculated, and a **report** is produced.
:::

:::compare Examples of anti-plagiarism resources
| Resource | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Turnitin** | Used by universities and schools worldwide; large archive of student papers; teachers see the report |
| **Antiplagiat** (antiplagiat.ru) | Widely used in Kazakhstan and Russia; checks Russian- and Kazakh-language texts |
| **Unicheck**, **Copyleaks**, **Plagscan** | Online checkers for schools and companies |
| **Grammarly Plagiarism Checker**, free online checkers | Quick checks against web pages (smaller databases) |
| **MOSS**, **JPlag** | Detect similar **program code**, even when variable names are changed |
| **Google search** | Searching an exact phrase in quotation marks shows whether it exists online |
:::

:::example Reading a similarity report
| Source | Match |
|---|---|
| 1. en.wikipedia.org — "Database normalization" | 12 % |
| 2. Student paper, NIS Almaty, 2025 | 9 % |
| 3. textbook "Computer Science 11", p. 87 | 4 % |
| **Total similarity** | **25 %** |

The highlighted text shows **which sentences** match. The teacher then checks whether each match is **properly quoted and cited** (acceptable) or **copied without acknowledgement** (plagiarism).
:::

:::cards
### Interpreting the score correctly
- A similarity score is **not** a plagiarism score — it only shows **matching text**.
- Correctly **quoted and referenced** passages, the **bibliography**, common phrases and technical terms also produce matches.
- Most checkers let the user **exclude quotes and the reference list** from the score.
- A **high score with no citations** needs investigation; even a **low score** can hide paraphrased plagiarism.
### Limitations of anti-plagiarism software
- Cannot detect copying from **sources not in its database** (printed books, private documents).
- Can be fooled by heavy **paraphrasing**, synonym-replacing tools or translation.
- Does not check **images, diagrams** or ideas well.
- **False positives** — common phrases, names, standard definitions.
- Uploaded work may be **stored** by the service — privacy and copyright of the student's own work.
- A human must always **interpret** the report.
:::

:::steps Checking your own work before submitting
- Use the school's checker (or a free one) on the **final draft**.
- Look at every **highlighted** passage.
- If it is a quotation — add **quotation marks** and a **reference**.
- If it is your paraphrase of a source — make sure the source is **cited**; rewrite more in your own words if it is too close.
- Check that the **reference list** contains every source.
:::

# Avoiding plagiarism
@lo 12.1.3.5 12.1.3.6

:::compare Quoting, paraphrasing and summarising
| Method | How | Citation needed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Quotation** | The **exact words**, in **quotation marks** | **Yes** — with the page if possible |
| **Paraphrase** | The idea rewritten **completely in your own words and structure** | **Yes** |
| **Summary** | The **main points** of a longer text, much shorter, in your own words | **Yes** |
| Common knowledge | Facts widely known (e.g. "Astana is the capital of Kazakhstan") | No |
:::

:::example Citing a source (Harvard / APA style)
In the text: *Normalisation reduces data redundancy (Connolly and Begg, 2015).*

In the reference list: *Connolly, T. and Begg, C. (2015) Database Systems. 6th edn. Harlow: Pearson.*

A web page: *Microsoft (2026) "Create a table of contents". Available at: support.microsoft.com (Accessed: 10 October 2026).*
:::

:::tip Good habits
Keep a list of every source **while you research** (author, title, date, URL), write notes **in your own words**, and use the word processor's **References** tools (citations, bibliography, footnotes).
:::

# Copyright protection rules
@lo 12.1.3.6

:::definition Copyright
The **exclusive legal right of the author** (or owner) of an original work — text, software, music, images, films, websites — to **copy, distribute, adapt, publish and perform** it, and to allow or forbid others to do so.
:::

:::compare The main rules of copyright protection
| Rule | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Copyright is automatic** | A work is protected **from the moment it is created** — no registration is needed; the © sign is optional but useful |
| **The author owns the rights** | **Economic rights** (to earn money from copies, licences) and **moral rights** (to be named as the author, to protect the work from distortion) |
| **Protection lasts for a limited time** | In Kazakhstan and many countries: the author's **life + 70 years**; afterwards the work enters the **public domain** |
| **Permission is needed** | To copy, publish, translate, adapt or sell a work you need the **owner's permission** or a **licence** |
| **Ideas and facts are not protected** | Copyright protects the **expression** (the text, code, image), not the idea itself |
| **Limited free use is allowed** | **Quotation** for study, criticism, news or teaching is allowed **with the author's name and source** and only to a justified extent |
| **Software is protected like a literary work** | Copying, distributing or installing software without a **licence** is illegal |
| **Licences set the conditions** | E.g. **Creative Commons** (BY — credit the author, NC — non-commercial, ND — no changes, SA — share alike); open-source licences (GPL, MIT) |
| **Breaking copyright has consequences** | Removal of content, fines, compensation to the owner, criminal liability for serious cases |
:::

:::callout info The law in Kazakhstan
Copyright is protected by the **Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan "On Copyright and Related Rights"** and the **Civil Code**. Computer programs and databases are protected works.
:::

:::compare Using other people's work in a school project
| You want to… | Allowed if… |
|---|---|
| Quote two sentences from a book | They are in **quotation marks** with a **reference** |
| Use a photo from a website | It has a **Creative Commons** or free licence (follow its conditions) or you have **permission** |
| Use a song in a video | It is **royalty-free / licensed**, or you have permission |
| Use code from an online tutorial | Its licence allows it, and you **credit** the author in comments |
| Use a classmate's work | **Never** as your own work |
:::

# Exam practice and summary
@lo 12.1.3.5 12.1.3.6

:::example Exam-style answers
**Q: Describe how anti-plagiarism software checks a document. [3]**
The text is **compared with a large database** of web pages, publications and submitted work (1); **matching fragments are highlighted** with links to their sources (1); a **similarity percentage / report** is produced (1).

**Q: Explain why a similarity score of 25% does not always mean that the student has plagiarised. [2]**
Matches may be **correctly quoted and cited** passages, the **reference list** or common phrases (1); the teacher must look at **which parts** match and whether they are acknowledged (1).

**Q: Name two copyright protection rules. [2]**
Copyright arises **automatically** when the work is created (1); a work may be **copied or published only with the owner's permission / licence**; quotations must name the **author and source**; protection lasts for the author's life + 70 years (1).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- Saying that changing a few words makes copied text your own — it is still plagiarism without a citation.
- Treating the similarity % as proof of plagiarism.
- Thinking that copyright must be **registered** — it is automatic.
- Thinking that everything on the Internet is in the **public domain**.
:::
